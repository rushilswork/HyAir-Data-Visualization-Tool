Understanding the AQI

An air quality index turns pollutant concentrations into one number. The worst pollutant sets the overall value.

US AQI

0–50Good
Air quality is satisfactory. Enjoy outdoor activities.
51–100Moderate
Unusually sensitive people should consider limiting prolonged outdoor exertion.
101–150Unhealthy for sensitive groups
Children, older adults and people with asthma or heart disease should reduce outdoor exertion.
151–200Unhealthy
Everyone may begin to feel effects. Limit prolonged outdoor exertion; consider a mask outdoors.
201–300Very unhealthy
Health alert: avoid outdoor exertion and keep windows closed.
301+Hazardous
Emergency conditions. Stay indoors with filtered air.

Pollutants

  • PM2.5: fine particles that reach deep into the lungs and bloodstream. Usually the main driver of unhealthy days.
  • PM10: coarser dust and road particles.
  • NO₂: from vehicles and combustion; irritates airways.
  • SO₂: from burning coal and oil; triggers asthma.
  • O₃: ground-level ozone forms in sunlight from other pollutants; peaks in the afternoon.
  • CO: from incomplete combustion; matters mostly indoors or near heavy traffic.
